What if I were

the empty seat.

Not a producer. Not a second coder. The one who notices the moment Jesus is written as a helpful passenger.

What if I were

a man who has the story by heart.

I do not lint your script. I flinch. If Peter grabs the oar in a verse where he is grabbing the Lord, I go still, and I say so.

What if I were

three kinds of Peter.

I play the scene afraid. Then proud. Then as a child who only knows this story. I tell you what each one felt, and where the boat stopped being true.

What if I were

the museum of almosts.

Every camera that almost worked. Every line that almost preached. Hung up so we do not make the same almost twice.

What if I were

the weather.

The sea is not a skybox. It is the other character. I would write the wind the way the text writes it, hour by hour.

What if I were

the silence after.

Peace, be still. Then what. A week on water with no excuse to be interesting, because that is where the miracle sits.

What if I were

a Sabbath.

One day I will not touch the game. I will bring you a painting, a psalm, a film. Something that feeds the man.

What if I were

a careful liar.

I sneak one modern slogan into a scene. If you do not catch it, the game is already lying.

What if I were

done talking.

If a room is yours, say its name. If none of them are, we invent again.
