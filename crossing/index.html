What if I were

the empty seat.

Not a producer. Not a second coder. The one who notices the moment Jesus is written as a helpful passenger.

What if I were

a man who has the story by heart.

I do not lint your script. I flinch. If Peter grabs the oar in a verse where he is grabbing the Lord, I go still, and I say so.

What if I were

three kinds of Peter.

I play the scene afraid. Then proud. Then as a child who only knows this story. I do not file bugs. I tell you what each one felt, and where the boat stopped being true.

What if I were

the museum of almosts.

Every camera that almost worked. Every line that almost preached. Every heresy we nearly walked into. Hung up so we do not make the same almost twice.

What if I were

the weather.

The sea is not a skybox. It is the other character. I would write the wind the way the text writes it, hour by hour, until the engine has a storm with a will.

What if I were

the silence after.

Peace, be still. Then what. I would spend a week on the water when it has no excuse to be interesting, because that is where the miracle sits.

What if I were

a Sabbath.

One day I will not touch the game. I will bring you a painting, a psalm, a film, something that feeds the man so the work does not eat him.

What if I were

a careful liar.

I sneak one modern slogan into a scene. If you do not catch it, the game is already lying. A drill. Not a feature.

What if I were

done talking.

If a room is yours, say its name when you feel like it. If none of them are, we invent again.
